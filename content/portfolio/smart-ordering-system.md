Modern transactional environments demand clarity under pressure. When queues build up during lunch hours, a point-of-sale interface must balance information density with unmistakable hit targets and clear payment feedback.

![High-fidelity register dashboard and real-time ordering screen](/images-/moka-pos-thumb.png)

## The Core Challenge

Existing legacy registers are cluttered with nested submenus, low-contrast text, and ambiguous button states. Cashiers were spending an average of 42 seconds per order simply hunting for modifiers and tax exemptions.

Our objective was to re-imagine the entire touch workflow from the ground up:

- Minimize tap depth for 90% of frequent items.
- Ensure high legibility under harsh overhead retail lighting.
- Provide instant visual confirmation upon payment success.

:portfolio-image{caption="Figure 1.0: Exploratory layout wireframes for handheld and tablet touch breakpoints" layout="wide" src="/images-/bu-01.png"}

## Modular Component Architecture

To support hundreds of merchant variations across F\&B and retail, we developed a strict token-based system. Color contrast meets WCAG AAA standards, and touch targets are standardized at a minimum of 48x48dp.

![Receipt breakdown and checkout completion screen](/images-/bu-02.png)

## Interactive States & Micro-interactions

Every state transition has intentional feedback. When a modifier is added, subtle haptics and spring animations communicate addition without obstructing the cashier's view of the next cart item.

![Tactile visual direction and cart drawer interactions](/images-/bu-03.png)

## Key Takeaways

By stripping away extraneous chrome and elevating typography and touch geometry, the new system compressed order completion times by 31% during pilot trials.
